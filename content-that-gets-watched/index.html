How to make videos people want to watch.

A free guide to what makes a video worth watching. Use the criteria on your next short video, then carry them into long form.

By Joshua Valentin, Social Money Models

Pick a video you want to make. Write down what someone would get from watching it.

As you read, use the criteria to work out how the video will deliver that. You'll have something specific to build toward.

Signed up for the guide? This is it.

Give the short video somewhere to go.

Make the premise clear from the opening.

The hook tells someone what they're watching and gives them a reason to stay. Choose a first frame that helps them see the premise.

Give them enough context to follow.

Once you've got their attention, give them what they need to understand what's happening. In a story, that can mean who is involved and what's at stake.

You know the background. They only have what you've shown them. Give them enough to care, then get into the idea.

Deliver the main idea simply.

This is where the video does what the opening set up. Make that part easy to follow.

Check the edit for stretches where very little happens. Look at what's on the screen, too. How much impact can you make with less clutter?

Give them a fresh reason to stay.

As the idea develops, introduce something that renews interest: a challenge, a reveal, something that changes what happens next.

Keep it connected to the video they're already watching. A random surprise can get attention while pulling them away from the point.

Make the ending worth reaching.

Go back to what you set up in the opening. Can you point to where the video delivers that promise?

A cliffhanger can leave a story open, but the video they've just watched still needs to give them something satisfying.

Give them a reason to stay, then deliver on it.

Look at how long people watch.

After you post, look at average view duration and average view percentage.

Average view duration tells you how long people stayed. Average view percentage tells you how much of the video they watched.

Read those beside the actual video. Where did you give people a reason to keep going? Where did the idea slow down or get harder to follow? Use the numbers to choose what you'll work on next.

Give a longer video more room to build.

With a video people click to watch, the promise starts in the title and thumbnail. Carry that promise into the hook. Give someone who clicks the video they came for.

Then give the story room. Introduce the people involved and what's at stake. Build the middle around the main premise, with something fresh to renew interest as it develops.

In the back half, explain what still needs explaining. Resolve the storylines you've opened and build toward a satisfying finish.

There's more time available, but the viewer still needs a reason to spend it with you.

Click-through rate adds a separate question: how often did someone choose your video when it was shown?

Read that separately from how long they stayed and how much they watched. The title and thumbnail can be getting one response while the video gets another. Knowing which part you're looking at helps you choose what to change.

Make the next video with something specific in mind.

Before you record, check your opening, context and main idea against the criteria. Plan what will renew interest and how the ending delivers what you set up.

After you post, look at how long people stayed and how much they watched. For a video people click to watch, check how often they chose it, too.

Write down what you're trying to improve. Keep those notes so you can come back to them with the video.
