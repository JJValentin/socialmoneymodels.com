Start with what the people watching you want to buy.

Give people a way to buy from you.

People can watch your content and still have no idea what they could buy from you.

An affiliate link or a digital product gives them something to click. Check whether it fits the people you've brought in and what they want help with.

Start with that connection before you build another thing to sell.

Connect your audience, trust and offer.

An ATM is audience, trust and monetization. Social Money Models is how I teach you to build yours.

Audience gets people paying attention. Trust gives them a reason to take what you say seriously. Monetization gives them a way to pay you for something they want.

Use that connection to choose an offer for the people watching you.

Choose something your audience wants.

Start with a need people already want help with.

Look at the people paying attention to you. What are they trying to get done? What have they already tried or paid for? Pay attention to the complaints about what they bought. Those tell you where help is still missing.

Put your skill beside that need. Match something they care about with something you can help them do.

Choose a result they can recognize.

A broad subject gives you too much to build. Pick a useful change someone can see when they've used what you sell. Write down what they'll be able to do or have at the end.

Keep the first offer focused on that change. Extra ideas can wait. Each thing you include should help the buyer get there.

Choose how you'll help them get it.

Think about what the buyer can do with the time and skills they have. A course or template can be a complete offer when they can use it to reach the result themselves.

If they need you to work through decisions with them or review their work, that changes what you're selling. If you're doing the work for them, say what you'll do. Match the help to the work you can deliver.

Check what the promise asks you to prove.

Build the promise around your experience and results. When you're testing a new outcome, tell buyers what you've delivered and what you're testing. Use other offers to understand the market, then make a promise you can deliver.

Put the price beside the work.

Look at what the buyer gets and what they'd choose instead. Then consider the work you'll do and the time you'll spend helping them after they buy. A price that works for a self-serve resource can mean something very different when you're personally reviewing every buyer's work.

Choose one useful result for the people you want to help. Make the delivery clear so they can decide whether they want it.

Say what someone can buy.

If you're selling a service, get the offer into one sentence. Say who you help, the outcome, the service and the price.

I help [specific person] [clear outcome] through [your service] for [price or clear quote basis].

Use the actual price you charge. If the job varies, state what determines the price.

If you're selling a digital product or membership, make the result and what they receive clear. Say who it's for. Make the price and any ongoing payment clear. Match your promise to the access and help you'll provide.

Start a conversation about the offer.

Tell your wider audience who you help and offer one useful thing related to that work.

Use a short guide, checklist or post you already have. Nothing yet? Write a one-page answer to a question those buyers ask you. Keep it focused on something they can use.

When someone asks for it, send it. Keep a record of what they ask next. Their questions give you something real to respond to.
